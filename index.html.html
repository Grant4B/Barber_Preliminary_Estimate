Scope of Work

What We're Building

The base scope has three parts: a structural rebuild of the second-floor sunroom, its conversion into a new full bathroom, and new structure and hardwood flooring across the entire second floor. An optional alternate layout is described at the end.

01

Sunroom Structural Rebuild

Structural

The existing 8' × 15' sunroom off the primary bedroom will be taken down to the structure and rebuilt as fully conditioned, insulated living space. Everything in this room is new — floor framing, walls, windows, and roof — so it performs like the rest of the house rather than a seasonal porch, and so it can reliably carry plumbing in the base configuration.

  • Demolition of the existing sunroom enclosure down to sound structure
  • New floor framing tied into the second-floor structure, insulated and sheathed
  • New insulated 2×6 exterior walls with sheathing, housewrap, and siding to match the house
  • New energy-efficient windows sized and placed during design
  • New roof assembly — framing, sheathing, membrane/shingle roofing, and flashing at the existing wall
  • Electrical rough-in, insulation throughout, and drywall ready for finishes
  • Dedicated mini-split head to condition the new space
02

New Full Bathroom in Rebuilt Sunroom

Full Gut / Plumbing

With the shell rebuilt, we'll fit out the new space as a full bathroom — a meaningful upgrade for a floor currently served by a single 5' × 8' bath. Because this is new plumbing territory, we'll run new supply, waste, and vent lines from the existing stacks, which we'll locate and confirm early in design.

  • New supply, waste, and vent plumbing routed to the rebuilt space
  • Full waterproofing and tile at the shower; tile flooring throughout
  • Vanity, toilet, and shower fixtures per selections made during design
  • Dedicated electrical circuits, lighting, GFCI outlets, and exhaust ventilation
  • In-floor heat available as a comfort upgrade — we'll price it during selections
03

Second-Floor Leveling & New Hardwood

Structural + Finish

Under the existing carpet, the second floor needs to be brought back to flat and solid before any new finish floor goes down. We'll remove the carpet across the floor, sister and level the framing across the full ~900 SF level, and install new subfloor and hardwood throughout — so every room on the floor reads as one continuous, quiet, level surface.

  • Removal and disposal of existing carpet and pad across approximately 775 SF
  • Asbestos abatement of existing flooring materials by a licensed abatement contractor, with air clearance testing
  • Sistering and leveling of the existing floor joists across the entire second floor
  • New plywood subfloor, glued and screwed, over the corrected framing
  • New site-finished hardwood flooring throughout the second floor
  • Doors trimmed and re-hung, base trim replaced or re-set as needed after the new floor height
  • Drywall repair and paint touch-up where leveling work opens walls or ceilings below
Optional Alternate Configuration
OPT

Two-Bathroom Layout — Primary Suite Annex

Optional

Because the rebuilt sunroom sits directly off the primary bedroom, there's a compelling alternate version of this project: open the rebuilt space into the bedroom to create a true primary suite, build the new full bathroom elsewhere on the floor, and enlarge and remodel the existing 5' × 8' bath. The result is a second floor with two bathrooms and a genuinely larger primary suite. The sunroom rebuild and the flooring scope carry over unchanged — this option redirects the bathroom work rather than adding a fourth project on top.

  • Rebuilt sunroom finished as living space and opened into the primary bedroom — a sitting area, dressing area, or expanded sleeping space, refined during design
  • New full bathroom constructed in an alternate location on the second floor, with new plumbing runs — siting to be studied against the existing stacks during design
  • Existing 5' × 8' bathroom enlarged and fully remodeled — new layout, tile, fixtures, and finishes
  • Second-floor leveling, subfloor, and hardwood scope identical to the base configuration

How Design Fees Work

Design services are billed hourly and scoped to your project. As a general budgeting guideline, design fees typically run approximately 10% of the estimated construction cost. Fees are billed in installments against a retainer, and any unearned balance is returned if the project doesn't move forward.

How We Work

Our Design + Build Process

We're not a contractor who shows up with a hammer on day one. We invest in the planning phase because that's where great projects are won or lost.

1

Design Development & Drawings

We'll develop detailed floor plans, elevations, and 3D views — including a side-by-side study of the base and two-bathroom configurations. You'll see the options clearly, and we'll refine together until the design truly reflects how you want the floor to live.

2

Selection & Specification

We guide you through all finish and fixture selections — tile, vanities, plumbing fixtures, hardwood species and finish, lighting, and hardware — keeping you within budget with clear allowances.

3

Permitting & Pre-Construction

We handle all DC permit applications, coordinate structural engineering for the sunroom rebuild and floor sistering, and finalize your construction contract with confirmed pricing before any work begins.

4

Construction & Regular Communication

Your dedicated project manager keeps you updated weekly — often daily — on progress, decisions needed, and scheduling. We work clean and respect your home.

5

Final Walkthrough & Closeout

We don't consider a project complete until you do. A formal punch list walkthrough ensures every detail is addressed, and we remain available for any warranty questions.
